# Foundations

Merit uses ink and stone: restrained blue actions on warm neutral surfaces, with
compact controls and comfortable reading space. Shared design tokens name the
colors, spacing, typography, and sizes used throughout the interface; themes supply
their values. Use the theme toolbar to compare the product and site themes.

These conventions apply Merit's experience principles across controls and patterns.
Component guidance owns detailed behavior; patterns explain how to use that language
in recurring situations.

## Restraint and context

Start with clear copy, alignment, and spacing. Use regular weight for ordinary
names and values, and reserve stronger treatment for the distinction someone
needs to notice. A new badge, icon, border, or panel should answer a specific
question; shared tokens alone do not make a composition coherent.

## Color and meaning

Use `action` for actionable emphasis and `action-subtle` for selection, including
navigation. Success, warning, and danger describe outcomes or concerns; keep
them distinct from selection and brand identity. A recommendation to fund is a
judgment, not a successful system action. Pair meaningful color with text or another cue.

Use surfaces, alignment, and spacing to establish hierarchy. Reserve shadows for
overlays. Keep controls distinguishable from surrounding surfaces and keep source
attribution readable even when it is secondary to the evidence.

## Typography and reading

Use `text-body` (14 px) for ordinary UI content, `text-detail` (13 px) for compact
lists, and `text-caption` (12 px) for supporting labels and metadata. Headings
establish structure through size, weight, and spacing rather than color alone.

Submitted material, assessments, reasoning, and multiline writing use
`text-reading` (15 px) with `leading-reading` (1.7). Limit prose to
`measure-reading` (65 characters) where the layout permits. Tables and reference
panels keep their compact typography. Align amounts with tabular numerals.
Buttons and fields share `text-control`: 13 px in the product and 14 px on the
site. The [field guidance](?path=/docs/components-fields--docs) explains labels and
feedback; [button examples](?path=/docs/components-button--docs) show action emphasis
and size variations.
Density follows the activity: finding work, reading evidence, comparing judgments,
editing, and authorizing changes can need different arrangements in one workspace.

## Spacing and grouping

Use the shared 4 px spacing scale to show relationships. Start with `space-1` or
`space-2` within a control or a compact action group, `space-3` or `space-4` between
related content, and `space-5` through `space-8` between larger groups or sections.
Choose the gap for the relationship and the activity; reading a review needs more
room than scanning its metadata. Preserve the tighter spacing inside shared forms.

Let the parent own the gap between its children. Account for the margins already
provided by headings and controls before adding another gap.
[Layout primitives](?path=/docs/components-layout-primitives--docs) apply this scale
to simple flows. [Page composition](?path=/docs/patterns-page-composition--docs)
explains how arrangements support reading, comparison, and editing; specialized
grids and scrolling stay with the feature that owns the task.

## Density

Desktop buttons and fields use a 32 px minimum height; small buttons keep that
height with smaller text and horizontal padding. Single-line tabs use 36 px.
Large buttons and button-shaped links use a 44 px minimum for prominent site
entry points. Coarse pointers get at least 44 px targets. Heights are minimums so enlarged text
and wrapping labels can grow without clipping.
Let content determine the space it needs. [Fields](?path=/docs/components-fields--docs)
and [forms](?path=/docs/patterns-form-dialog--docs) own their internal spacing and
feedback arrangements while preserving these shared target sizes.

## Motion

Buttons distinguish hover from press with surface changes, keeping their labels
and position steady. Pending actions retain their color and show a spinner;
icon-only actions replace their glyph with the spinner. Unavailable actions use
muted text and neutral surfaces so progress remains distinct from unavailability.

Use brief transitions to make opening and closing a working surface feel continuous.
Keep focus and input available during the entrance, preserve the surface throughout
its exit, and skip transitions when the person prefers reduced motion.
[Dialogs](?path=/docs/components-dialog--docs) own their entrance, exit, and
content-retention behavior.

## Focus

Shared controls integrate focus into their appearance.
Keyboard focus underlines action labels; secondary and quiet actions also receive
the subtle action fill. Icon-only buttons use an inset bottom marker in their
foreground color. Button-shaped links underline, while navigation and menu options
use a side marker so keyboard focus remains distinct from the current selection.

Editable text fields and select triggers show an ink border, faint tinted fill,
and inset bottom marker whenever focused, including pointer editing and restored
focus after a select closes. Currency prefixes share the field's boundary. Invalid
fields keep danger-colored borders and focus markers. Read-only fields remain
focusable for selection and copying, retain their muted surface, and use a quiet
dashed outline instead of the editing underline. Disabled controls are skipped.
Focus remains visible alongside each state; it must not make a fixed value look editable.

Tab labels use a tinted fill and text underline, retaining the separate selection
underline. Focused scrolling panels use an inset side marker. React Aria supplies
the control state attributes; native links use `:focus-visible`. Native content
without a specialized treatment retains the ink-colored outline fallback, including
links and summaries. Remove that outline only when the appearance supplies a visible
replacement: an underline on a link does not reach nested flex content such as the
brand or a person identity.

Keep indicators visible without moving content or clipping them in scroll areas.
Forced-colors mode uses system outlines because fills and shadows may be suppressed.

Foundations / Focus compares the control family with real keyboard and pointer
interaction. Use both themes and check hover, invalid and read-only states,
forced colors, and clipping when changing the focus convention.
