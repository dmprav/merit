# Product strategy

Merit helps people carry grant work forward while keeping source material,
judgments, and resulting actions understandable and traceable. Its direction is
to reduce the effort of preparing and coordinating work while preserving the
information people need to exercise judgment and account for their choices.

## Current purpose and working context

Merit is currently a portfolio and learning project. The public website and
fictional demo let potential employers and other visitors assess product judgment
through use. Invited workspaces support exploration with sample data; Merit is
not offered as a live grant-management service. The
[access workflow](../workflows/public-demo-and-access.md) explains that boundary.

The working context is a program manager at a small independent foundation,
preparing applications, received assessments, and funding recommendations for
committee discussion. This gives development concrete people, information, and
consequences to reason about. It is a starting context for exploration; the
audience and broader coverage remain open.

## Enduring commitments

Keep source material distinguishable from interpretations and judgments, preserve
attribution and relevant versions, and make missing information and disagreements
visible. People should understand what an action commits, who has authority to
carry it out, and how to recover when work changes or an operation fails. The
[domain concepts](../domain/concepts.md) define these distinctions.

Capabilities should deliver useful work within existing processes and fit together
as a coherent application. Web and MCP should preserve the same business meaning
and guarantees through interactions suited to each interface. These commitments
guide choices as the audience, capabilities, and technology evolve.

## Choose work and resolve trade-offs

Choose work that improves a meaningful grant task, makes the product's behavior
understandable through use, or answers a consequential product or technical
question. Explain the intended benefit and the assumption being tested before
choosing the scope. Complete the affected behavior, experience, and documentation
so the result can be evaluated as part of the application.

Learning can motivate a technology choice or an experiment. Retain it according
to what it enables and the ongoing cost of understanding, operating, and changing
the product. The [decision records](../decisions/README.md) explain current choices
and when to reconsider them. Demonstration and learning must preserve the
commitments above: a convincing example accurately represents available behavior,
and fictional or prepared content remains identifiable.

Weigh immediate effort against the cost of future use and change. Substantial
design or restructuring is justified when it clarifies ownership, removes
recurring complexity, or establishes behavior needed across capabilities. Let
demonstrated needs and explicit hypotheses guide flexibility and scope.

## Build a connected application

The focus is an application people can find their way around, use, and return to.
Develop the shared product structure and everyday interactions so individual
features feel like parts of one working environment. The
[experience principles](experience.md) guide those choices.

The demo is a way to experience the product. It opens a workspace with fictional
data and uses the product's layouts and interactions. The
[demo workflow](../workflows/public-demo-and-access.md) explains how visitors
enter, explore, and keep their progress.

## Be useful wherever teams are in their process

Teams should be able to use Merit for a meaningful part of their grant work with
minimal setup and disruption. Design around the information they already have
and the places their work goes next, so each supported task delivers value within
their existing process.

The adoption hypothesis is that teams could start with one useful part of their
work and choose more of Merit as the capabilities prove valuable together. Test
the entry and handoff as well as the work inside Merit: understand what information
people already have, what must be prepared, and where the result goes next.

## Use grant work to develop the experience

The committee-preparation example develops the working context through changed
evidence, differing reviewer judgments, and budget trade-offs.

Use this example to improve the shared product experience. The
[grantmaking workflows](../workflows/review-and-decide.md) describe current behavior,
with [future committee work](../workflows/committee-decisions.md) recorded separately.
Broader product scope remains open.

## Judge product quality

Evaluate the current demo through believable grant work. Visitors should be able to:

- Find useful work without an explanation from the project owner.
- Move between overview, detail, and editing without losing context.
- Understand the results of their actions and resume saved work.

Practical adoption needs evidence from grants teams using their own processes.
Investigate whether Merit reduces effort while keeping source material traceable,
uncertainty and disagreements visible, and decisions accountable. A successful
fictional example establishes what the product can demonstrate; real use is needed
to assess whether the approach fits a team's work.

## Open questions

The detailed audience model, coverage of the grant lifecycle, and delivery
milestones remain open. The relative needs of foundation staff, reviewers,
applicants, and recipients need further investigation. The
[domain guide](../domain/README.md) describes varied practices and the limits of
the evidence gathered so far.

Start an investigation with the decision it should inform, the assumption that
matters, and who holds the missing knowledge. Use accounts of actual work and
relevant artifacts to understand constraints, handoffs, and consequences. Record
what the evidence supports, what remains uncertain, and what would change the
current choice. Findings should update the owning concepts or decisions; GitHub
issues and milestones hold delivery scope and progress.
