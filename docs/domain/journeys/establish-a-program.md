# Establish a funding program

A funder turns a mission, community need, or policy objective into a funding
approach. Program staff, governing leaders, finance colleagues, and community or
subject experts help decide what to support and how; see [people](../people.md).

## Set the purpose and resources

Before seeking applications, the funder considers the change it wants, whose
needs matter, what previous funding has taught it, and whether grants are an
appropriate response. Available resources and evidence of progress shape the
approach. For example, [UK central-government guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/government-functional-standard-govs-015-grants/guidance-for-general-grants-html)
places consultation, a business case, and evaluation planning in program design.

The funder defines whom it will support, for what work, over what period, and
within what budget. These choices shape eligibility, assessment criteria,
application effort, and later reporting. In US federal grants, the
[funding announcement](https://grants.gov/learn-grants/grants-101/pre-award-phase)
gives applicants the requirements needed to judge eligibility and fit.

## Make the opportunity accessible

Access can begin with invitations or a public call. [Ford identifies most of its
grants itself](https://www.fordfoundation.org/work/our-grants/grant-opportunities/),
with selection varying by program. [UK government competition guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/government-functional-standard-govs-015-grants/5-competition-for-funding-html)
starts from competition, with justified exceptions for direct awards.

The resulting opportunity communicates purpose, funding, eligibility, timing,
selection responsibilities, and expectations after an award. Applicants can
[seek funding](seek-funding.md), while the funder prepares to
[select recipients](select-and-award.md). A recurring round is one way to organize
a program; see [concepts](../concepts.md).

Questions to investigate: Who shapes the priorities? Which eligible groups might
never hear about the opportunity? What application and reporting effort is
proportionate to the support offered?
