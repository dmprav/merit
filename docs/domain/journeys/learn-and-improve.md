# Learn across grants and shape future funding

Learning helps people decide what to continue, change, or stop. It begins in
program design and continues through delivery and after grants end. The UK's
[Magenta Book](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-magenta-book/magenta-book-central-government-guidance-on-evaluation-html)
places evaluation before, during, and after an intervention, serving learning
and accountability.

## Decide what is useful to know

Recipients, funders, evaluators, and affected people bring different questions.
A delivery team may need to improve participation; a funder may reconsider its
strategy. The intended decisions shape the evidence, timing, and people involved.

[Activities, outputs, and outcomes](../concepts.md#evidence-and-results) answer
different questions. Providing training, counting participants who complete it,
and observing changes in their employment are different observations. Improved
employment alone does not establish that the grant caused it:
[HM Treasury's impact-evaluation guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-magenta-book/quality-in-policy-impact-evaluation-qpie-html)
distinguishes measuring change from establishing attribution.

## Interpret findings and act

Checking one recipient's commitments differs from examining a portfolio or funding
strategy. [MacArthur evaluates portfolios and strategy rather than individual
grants](https://www.macfound.org/learning/evaluation-strategy), emphasizing contribution
rather than attribution. Compliant delivery alone cannot answer every strategic
question.

Recipient and community experience can expose effects that reports miss. The
[Packard Foundation](https://www.packard.org/about/evaluation-and-learning/)
involves grantees and affected people in designing, interpreting, and sharing
learning. Its approach includes acting on feedback, returning findings, and
respecting participants' privacy and trust.

Findings can change [active delivery](deliver-and-manage.md), support, reporting,
or [future funding priorities](establish-a-program.md).

Questions to investigate: Who defines success? Can recipients speak candidly when
future funding is at stake? Which findings are comparable across grants, and
which depend on local context?
