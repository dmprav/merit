# Deliver and manage a grant

The recipient carries out the funded work while the funder provides money,
support, and oversight. Delivery and finance teams need the agreement's purpose,
amount, timetable, conditions, and reporting obligations. The
[US federal grant lifecycle](https://grants.gov/learn-grants/grants-101/the-grant-lifecycle)
illustrates this transition from award to implementation.

## Coordinate work, money, and evidence

The recipient plans work and spending, coordinates partners, and gathers evidence.
The funder arranges payments, reviews information, and responds to problems.
Awarded, paid, and spent amounts differ: cash may arrive before work or reimburse
costs already incurred. For example,
[Wellcome normally reimburses spending quarterly](https://wellcome.org/research-funding/guidance/how-to-claim-funds-from-Wellcome/grant-payments).

Reporting connects what happened, what was spent, what changed, and what needs
attention. It can prompt support or adjustment, through dialogue or prescribed
financial and programmatic reports. See [context](../context.md) for different
approaches to the funding relationship.

## Adapt or bring the grant to an end

Circumstances can change the timetable, team, institution, or plan. Requesting,
approving, and implementing a change are separate steps. In Australian research
funding, [NHMRC's variations policy](https://www.nhmrc.gov.au/about-us/resources/grant-variations-policy)
covers delays, extensions, transfers, and relinquishment. Approved changes can
affect payment and reporting milestones.

Finishing delivery can leave reports, payments, repayments, or questions to settle.
The [US Congressional Research Service's closeout overview](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/IF/PDF/IF13106/IF13106.1.pdf)
distinguishes recipient submissions from the agency's review and financial
reconciliation. Ending early also creates final reporting responsibilities, as
NHMRC's policy shows. Evidence from delivery feeds into [learning](learn-and-improve.md).

Questions to investigate: Which changes can recipients make themselves? Who can
commit the funder to revised terms? How do delivery and finance teams resolve
conflicting accounts of progress?
