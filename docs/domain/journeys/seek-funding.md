# Seek and secure funding

Preparing an application can involve project leads, fundraising or research staff,
finance colleagues, leadership, and partners. A small organization may combine
these [responsibilities](../people.md).

## Decide whether to apply

The applicant compares its purpose, eligibility, capacity, and timing with the
funder's offer. Preparing a proposal takes effort before funding is certain.
[US federal guidance](https://grants.gov/learn-grants/grants-101/pre-award-phase)
asks applicants to assess their capabilities against the requirements and
complete relevant registrations before submission.

A proposal explains why the work matters, what will happen, who will contribute,
what it will cost, and what support is sought. A preliminary inquiry may precede
it: at the [John Templeton Foundation](https://www.templeton.org/grants/apply-for-grant),
only invited applicants advance from a short inquiry to a full proposal.
Clarifications or revisions may follow.

## Follow the request to an outcome

After submission, the applicant answers questions, coordinates colleagues, and
considers whether the request still makes sense. Withdrawal differs from rejection.
For example, [NSF's non-award procedures](https://www.nsf.gov/policies/pappg/24-1/ch-4-non-award-decisions-transactions)
allow applicant withdrawal before a funding recommendation; funding from another
sponsor can also lead a proposal to leave consideration.

After rejection, the applicant may revise, seek another funder, reduce the work,
or stop. Feedback is not always available: the [Parker Foundation](https://theparkerfoundation.org/grant-making/grant-seekers/application-process-online/)
describes demand exceeding its budget and an inability to provide individual
feedback. A decline need not mean the work lacked value.

An offer brings a different choice: whether the amount, obligations, and proposed
work are feasible together. [Agreeing the award](select-and-award.md) establishes
the commitments under which the applicant becomes a recipient.

Questions to investigate: Who authorizes the request? How do partial funding or
delayed decisions change the plan?
