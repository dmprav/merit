# Context and variation

This guide draws on public, philanthropic, community, and research funding in
the US, UK, EU, and Australia. It shows several ways of working, rather than their
prevalence across the sector.

## What shapes the work

| Dimension      | Meaningful differences                                                                            |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Access         | Open applications, invitations, or targeted outreach.                                             |
| Selection      | Competitive comparison or noncompetitive allocation.                                              |
| Support        | A defined project or an organization's ongoing work.                                              |
| Timing         | Deadlines, rolling intake, staged applications, and multi-year awards.                            |
| Delivery       | One recipient, a partnership, a consortium, or an intermediary distributing funds onward.         |
| Accountability | Evidence, approvals, reporting, and payment terms set by the funder, agreement, and jurisdiction. |

[Houston Endowment](https://houstonendowment.org/grantmaking/invited-applicants)
uses invitations and distinguishes operating support, project support, and fiscally
sponsored work. [NIFA](https://www.nifa.usda.gov/grants/lifecycle) describes both
competitive and noncompetitive funding.

The [Trust-Based Philanthropy Project](https://www.trustbasedphilanthropy.org/practices)
advocates unrestricted multi-year support, simpler paperwork, and recipient
feedback.

## Organizations and relationships

The recipient organization, delivery lead, and people intended to benefit can be
different parties. In collaborative EU projects, a coordinator connects participating
organizations with the funder.
([European Commission project guidance](https://commission.europa.eu/funding-and-tenders/managing-your-project/managing-your-project-under-grant-agreement_en))

The sources favor established institutions. Smaller and volunteer-led
organizations, unsuccessful applicants, participatory decisions, intermediaries,
and other jurisdictions need more direct evidence.
