# Verification record

## Behavioral decisions

- Every change to who can read, write, or run something was listed in full and never capped: row-level security, policies, roles, views, ownership, default privileges, disabled triggers, `ALTER FUNCTION`, security-definer functions and procedures, grants, and `DO` blocks.
- A `DO` block was quoted with the SQL its `EXECUTE` calls and body run. One changed in place also named the literals it gained or lost, such as a table added to its policy loop.
- Grants were compared per privilege, column, object, role, and grant option, then regrouped into statements. A grant that did not break down, such as a role membership, was listed whole.
- A statement's effect followed its verb: a `DROP TABLE` or `DROP COLUMN` added in a new migration counted as a removal.
- Each table got one line counting the columns, constraints, indexes, and triggers it gained, lost, or changed, including items edited inside its `CREATE TABLE`. Dropped tables came first, then new tables, and tables after the twelfth were counted.
- An added and a removed statement with the same summary were shown once as changed in place (`~`), with a legend line. A function that gained or lost `SECURITY DEFINER` said which.
- Statements no pattern recognized, such as a data backfill, were all listed.
- Base and head compared the combined migrations and runtime grants, so a grant moving between the two files was no change.
